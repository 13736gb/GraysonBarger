Wind Tunnel Airfoil Cross Section

Background

As a research assistant with the GIMAS Lab at Penn State, I worked on a project studying how synthetic jet actuators affect flow around airfoils.

Goal

Design an airfoil cross-section model that mounts in a wind tunnel and has holes for pressure taps, so pressure can be measured at various points along the airfoil.

Challenges

  • Making room for the pressure taps while still being able to secure the airfoil to the wind tunnel walls.
  • Modeling a complex shape like an airfoil, which I had never done before.
  • Working within the manufacturing constraints set by the engineering services company that produced the part.

Solution / Results

I learned to define a shape from coordinates and used that method to model a NACA 0015 airfoil. The model mounts to the wind tunnel walls with an adjustable angle of attack. I optimized the pressure tap pattern, with 10 taps on the top surface and 10 on the bottom, while minimizing the amount of material used in the cross section. I designed the center section, and additional sections attach to its sides to span the full width of the test section.
